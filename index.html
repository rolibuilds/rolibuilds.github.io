  1. Making repetitive work repeatable

    PowerPoint VBA for customer kick-off decks

    From
    Customer kick-off decks assembled manually, with differences in content and formatting
    Automation
    PowerPoint VBA populated and formatted a standard presentation structure using customer- and implementation-specific information
    To
    A consistent, ready-to-review kick-off deck produced in around 30 minutes instead of four to five hours

    Customer kick-off presentations were prepared manually by two implementation teams, leading to differences in content and formatting. I built a PowerPoint VBA solution that made the process faster and gave both teams a consistent starting point.

    The kick-off meeting was the first formal meeting with the customer and an important first impression at the beginning of each billing migration.

    Two implementation teams prepared these presentations, and each person built the deck manually. The content and formatting weren’t always consistent, while the same types of customer, market, timeline and implementation information had to be added each time.

    I built a PowerPoint VBA solution that used structured source information to create much of the presentation automatically. This made deck preparation faster and introduced a more consistent starting point for both teams, while still leaving room to review and adapt the presentation for each customer.

    Preparation time fell from approximately four to five hours to around 30 minutes per deck.

  2. Automating the monthly cycle

    Excel VBA for recurring financial reporting

    From
    Two working days of repetitive monthly report preparation
    Automation
    Excel VBA handled most of the data preparation and created the report’s PivotTables
    To
    A repeatable process completed in approximately half a day

    A monthly financial report spread across more than 20 worksheets took around two working days to prepare. I taught myself Excel VBA and automated most of the repetitive work.

    At OTP Bank, I inherited a monthly financial report spread across more than 20 Excel worksheets. Preparing it took around two working days each month. The process had worked this way for years, but that didn’t feel like a good enough reason to keep doing it manually.

    The work started with SAP source reports and continued through several repetitive preparation steps before the PivotTables and management charts could be produced.

    I taught myself Excel VBA with a lot of help from Google searches and the Stack Overflow community. VBA took over most of the repetitive preparation and created the PivotTables, leaving the final charts and review for me.

    The monthly effort fell to approximately half a day, and another controlling team later adopted the solution for its own reporting.

  3. Moving beyond the spreadsheet

    From Excel reporting to Power BI

    From
    A VBA-supported Excel report spread across more than 20 worksheets
    Transformation
    The existing financial logic and management requirements redesigned for an interactive Power BI dashboard
    To
    A more focused management report with a recurring update process of around 30 minutes

    The VBA solution made reporting faster, but the workbook still needed regular maintenance. I initiated the move to Power BI and represented the business side throughout the transformation.

    The VBA solution made the monthly process much faster, but the report still lived in an Excel workbook with more than 20 worksheets. It needed regular maintenance, and I felt we had reached the point where further automation would only improve the existing format rather than move beyond it.

    When I discovered Power BI, I saw an opportunity to rethink the same report.

    Before we started designing the new solution, I worked directly with the managing director to understand what information was actually needed. The aim was to focus on the figures and trends that supported management decisions and remove everything that added little value.

    I initiated the move to Power BI and represented the business side throughout the project. I translated the existing financial logic into requirements, contributed to the dashboard design and tested the new output against the established report. Once the figures and analytical logic were working as intended, I provided business acceptance. The technical development was carried out by an external specialist.

    Preparing the source files, loading the data and refreshing the dashboard took around 30 minutes. More importantly, the solution turned the same underlying information into something more visual, easier to explore and better aligned with management needs.

My flagship project

Corporate Services Automation Platform

CSAP is my personal portfolio project, built around a realistic business problem: how to manage requests that require both approval and financial control without relying on disconnected emails and spreadsheets.

It uses Dataverse and Power Automate to connect structured request data, financial validation, exchange rates, budget controls and approval workflows in one traceable process.

My aim with CSAP is to gain hands-on, practical experience by building a focused MVP that is understandable, testable and useful.

Current capability

  • Financial validation
  • Multi-currency handling
  • FX-rate maintenance
  • Budget-capacity checks
  • Approval routing
  • Clarification and exception handling
  • Traceable approval history

Project principle

Human-led and built. AI-supported. Tested in practice.

When CSAP began, my practical Power Platform experience was still very limited. The project moved me from guided exercises and concepts to a much larger AI-supported build, where I make the design decisions, implement the solution, test the results and remain responsible for what we build.

I worked through the requirements, process design, data model and financial rules before building the Dataverse foundation and Power Automate workflows. Testing has been part of the work from the beginning, and I’ve changed both the design and the scope when the evidence showed that I should.

Building this as a private individual comes with technical limitations. I treat those as design constraints rather than excuses: simplify where it makes sense, find practical ways to test the important controls and clearly document what couldn’t be validated in the same way as it would be in a full organisational environment.

My role

  • Business analysis and requirements definition
  • Financial-process and control design
  • Dataverse data modelling and implementation
  • Power Automate workflow design and build
  • Runtime and exception testing
  • Scope decisions and documentation
  • Final responsibility for the working solution
