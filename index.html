Automation Journey

A few small experiments gradually became a professional direction.

  1. task automation
  2. reporting automation
  3. reporting transformation
  4. process automation

  1. Making repetitive work repeatable

    PowerPoint VBA for customer kick-off decks

    From
    Customer kick-off decks assembled manually, with differences in content and formatting
    Automation
    PowerPoint VBA populated and formatted a standard presentation structure using customer- and implementation-specific information
    To
    A consistent, ready-to-review kick-off deck: from 4-5 hours to about 30 minutes per deck, roughly 50 decks a year on my own workload, so around 200 hours a year. Adopted by the team after a pilot.

    Customer kick-off decks that took four to five hours to build by hand are generated from structured data in around 30 minutes.

  2. Automating the monthly cycle

    Excel VBA for the monthly financial report

    From
    Two working days of repetitive monthly report preparation
    Automation
    Excel VBA handled most of the data preparation and created the report’s PivotTables
    To
    A repeatable process completed in approximately half a day

    A monthly report of more than 20 worksheets that took two working days now takes about half a day, which saves roughly 18 working days a year. Another controlling team adopted it.

  3. Moving beyond the spreadsheet

    From Excel to Power BI

    From
    A VBA-supported Excel report spread across more than 20 worksheets
    Transformation
    The existing financial logic and management requirements redesigned for an interactive Power BI dashboard
    To
    A more focused management report with a recurring update process of around 30 minutes

    I initiated the move and owned the business side end to end: I interviewed the Managing Director, specified the requirements, designed the dashboard, then tested and accepted it. An external specialist built it. Refreshing it takes around 30 minutes.

  4. The next step

    Building CSAP on Power Platform

    From
    Training exercises and concepts
    Automation
    A much larger build: my idea and business design, Copilot's step-by-step guidance, my hands-on implementation and testing
    To
    A working, tested approval workflow with guardrails; the rest of the MVP is still in progress

    Hands-on exercises at an OTP LAB open day showed me what Power Platform could do for finance processes, so I started building my own use case: the Corporate Services Automation Platform (CSAP), below.

Corporate Services Automation Platform (CSAP)

CSAP is my personal portfolio project, built around a realistic business problem: how to manage requests that require both approval and financial control without relying on disconnected emails and spreadsheets. It uses Dataverse and Power Automate to connect structured request data, financial validation, exchange rates, budget controls and approval workflows in one traceable process.

Current capability

  • Financial validation
  • Approval routing
  • Budget-capacity checks
  • Traceable approval history

How I worked with AI

Built with AI assistance: Copilot guided the build and drafted most test cases. The business design, financial rules, scope decisions and acceptance testing were mine; I corrected scope drift and verified that the controls hold in the automation itself, not only in the interface.

Controls
approval-authority and security guards, duplicate protection, pending funding holds, final financial revalidation; official Hungarian National Bank (MNB) exchange rates for EUR, USD, GBP and CHF
Built with
Dataverse, Power Automate, Power Apps (Dataverse views)
Environment
personal environment with fictional test data; I tested the important controls in practice and documented what could not be validated as it would be in an organisational environment

The MVP is still in progress.
